Directions

After being placed in your family units, you will hold a family meeting where you need to create a monthly budget. This budget needs to be based on your family's income, profile, and specific needs. Some of you will face difficult decisions when it comes to what your family truly needs each month. After you have established your monthly budget, you will draw a "Life Happens" card. This card will reveal the "hand" that life has dealt for you. You will then need to rework your budget. When finished with your updated budget, draw another card and rework the budget again. You will draw one last card and rework your budget and attempt to accommodate the events and circumstances of these cards.

The Objectives

  1. For families to provide their members with the basic necessities of: food, clothing, shelter, taxes.
  2. For families to provide their children with the best possible education.
  3. To maintain the physical and mental well-being of each family member by providing: health care, entertainment, vacations.

Calculating Your Budget

  • Be sure to accommodate all mandatory expenditures.
  • Consider how your family is preparing for any unexpected expenses.

During the Exercise

  • Each time you receive a "Life Happens" card, consider how the event impacts your budget and do your best to accommodate it.
  • When you get a new card, assume it is a new month (i.e., you just got paid).
  • As you experience different "Life Happens" events, discuss how the event impacts your family's quality of life.
  • Throughout the exercise, discuss: How is your family doing? Are you happy? Stressed out? "Successful," or merely getting by? What, if anything, needs to change to help you do better?

A note about the families: income levels are based on data from the U.S. Census Bureau's "Income in the United States: 2024" report (Current Population Survey, 2025 Annual Social and Economic Supplement, released September 2025). In 2024, median U.S. household income was $83,730. Households in the lowest fifth (quintile) earned $34,510 or less; the second quintile ran up to $65,100; the third quintile up to $105,500; and the fourth quintile up to $175,700, with the top fifth earning more than that. The families below are placed at different points across that range and represent "typical" incomes for the occupations described. None of the families meet official definitions of poor or marginally poor, so they do not qualify for social services.

Reflection

The activity ends after your family has had adequate time to manage its budget for three months and deal with the particular life circumstances determined by your profile and "Life Happens" cards. Write a reflection about your family's experience: what life events happened, how you dealt with them, and what the impact was on your family. Answer the following questions:

  1. What do you think about this scenario?
  2. How did each of your budgets compare with each other?
  3. How can you explain this through a sociological lens, rather than a personal one?
